What we actually don't understand

Eleven questions, but underneath them there is one first-level question and one second-level question. Everything else follows from the answers.

  1. FIRST LEVEL — does the winner get the 92-dealer network contract, or become the preferred supplier for the network? Mandatory (like the physical shop fit-out: every STIHL SHOP has to have its digital presence on UNIFY) vs. recommended (a good word from STIHL, then we are on our own). Difference: 92 × our price vs. $0 guaranteed. (→ CQ-01)
  2. SECOND LEVEL — who pays F925, and who signs? Head office, the dealer, or a hybrid. It only becomes real once the first level is answered. (→ CQ-02)

Our position, for the three of us (not sent to STIHL): F925 does not sell. We do not publish, prospect, or go looking for clients. Whether the programme is mandatory or optional, F925's job is to get the dealer(s) ready to be onboarded and run them well afterwards. We assist STIHL on the technical side of bringing dealers in; we do not assist a pre-dealer dealer who has not decided yet. Adoption is STIHL's job. Write the proposal that way.

An idea for pricing (15 Sep — for discussion, nothing decided): because we don't know which answer we'll get, shape §9 so it works either way — one product, two ways to buy it: the dealer-direct price we agreed ($2,500 + $600/mo) with volume bands, plus a network-programme rate that applies if STIHL adopts the framework as a brand standard, whoever is invoiced. The deal CQ-01–02 · what we are bidding for, and for whom

CQ-01

Brand standard or optional programme

What we don't understand: whether winning means the 92-dealer network (STIHL mandates UNIFY the way it mandates the shop fit-out) or "preferred supplier" (a recommendation, and we are on our own). Difference: 92 × our price vs. $0 guaranteed.

Why it matters: it is the whole business case — and it decides how we write §2 Strategy, §8 Cost and §9 Rollout. We ask about the mechanism (brand standard vs. programme, timeline, expected adoption) rather than "will you force your dealers?", because that question invites a "no". "100% by end of 2027" is a mandate in everything but name; HQ never pays for something optional.

Why it matters: contract, invoicing and the onboarding flow. Second level: it only becomes real once CQ-01 is answered. Either way F925 does not go and find dealers; STIHL hands them to us ready to onboard.

Ownership & the long run CQ-04–05 · what we keep, what they keep, what the dealer keeps

CQ-04

Platform ownership, contract term, and what happens at the end

What we don't understand: whether F925 stays the owner of UNIFY (licensed to use the STIHL brand) or STIHL wants to own the platform with us running it as a service — and, either way, how long the deal runs and what a dealer is left with if it ends.

Why it matters: this is the single biggest commercial question: it changes the price, what we are willing to build, MARTIN-3's IP position, and Mike's legal review (D-xmtwhacfx).

CQ-05

Domains & DNS — who owns them, who can change them

What we don't understand: the pain point in The Process Step 4. Some dealers have a domain, some don't. For the ones that don't — do we register it and who owns and pays for it? For the ones that do — do we get registrar access or do they make the DNS change themselves?

Why it matters: at ~90 cutovers this is the biggest operational coordination point of the rollout; whatever the answer, the process has to be painless for the dealer.

Brand CQ-06 · the guardrails

CQ-06

Point of contact, template sign off and brand assets

What we don't understand: whether STIHL will hand us the official kit (logos, licensed type, approved imagery) and formally sign off the template — or whether "on brand" is left to our judgement and challenged later. And who settles a dispute over a dealer's local content.

Why it matters: Governance is 20% of the score; a defined certification step is evidence for §5, and it protects us from re-doing the template after the pilot.
