01

Verdict

Yes, with four changes. The architecture already treats 100 dealers as 100 domains on one shared site application, not 100 separate deployments, so adding a dealer costs a DNS record and a database row. The public traffic path has real headroom today. The parts that do not survive a 100-dealer rollout untouched are image uploads on the platform instance, the free email tier on onboarding days, the free AI and weather quotas behind the “shop is open” widget, and the absence of any monitoring.

02

What runs where

Two DigitalOcean App Platform services in Sydney, one Postgres database, one Spaces bucket, and a ring of third-party APIs. Every dealer domain points at the same site application, which asks the platform for that dealer’s content by hostname and caches it for 30 seconds.

Two things follow from this shape. First, the dealer-site instance is the customer-facing bottleneck: every page of every dealer is rendered on that one shared vCPU, and nothing is cached at the edge except images. Second, the platform instance is a shared dependency: if it is busy encoding a dealer’s photos, the 30-second cache is all that stands between a slow upload and slow pages on every dealer site.

Test results

Two test beds. Production was hit read-only (GET only, no emails, no uploads) from Santiago, so absolute latency carries ≈ 500 ms of distance; throughput and error counts are the signal. Local was a fresh clone of both repos, the platform capped at 800 MB heap, a copy of the dev database seeded to 106 dealers (with domains, five change requests and five messages each), and the site pointed at it. Local CPU is Apple Silicon; the production run showed a DO shared vCPU delivers about 1/4.6 of it (5.4 ms local per homepage predicted 40 pages/s; production measured 34–36).

05

Changes before 100 dealers

Ordered by what breaks first. The first three are needed before the pilot in December; the rest before the January rollout. Flag the ones you’ll own, comment where you disagree with the priority.

1
Take image derivatives off the request pathcritical

uploadDealerAsset() in platform/server/spaces.ts runs 24 sharp encodes before answering. Return after the original is stored, and generate derivatives in a queue with concurrency 1 (a setImmediate loop over a table of pending keys is enough at this scale). The site’s /img proxy already falls back to on-demand resizing, so nothing breaks while a derivative is missing. Also resize the platform to apps-s-1vcpu-2gb or apps-d-1vcpu-2gb.

2
Resend Procritical

The free plan’s 100 emails per day is below one onboarding day (100 invites plus 100 OTP logins). NZ$33/month.

3
Monitoring, alerts, error trackingcritical

Uptime checks on all dealer domains plus both health endpoints; DO alert rules for CPU, memory and restarts; an error tracker in both apps. These are runbook items I-01 to I-03 and the RFP scores them.

4
Second dealer-site instance with request-based autoscalinghigh

Today one deploy, one crash or one crawler burst affects all 100 sites at once. Two instances (min 2, max 4) give zero-downtime deploys and ~70 pages/s. Dedicated CPU also unlocks CPU-based autoscaling.

5
Rate-limit the auth and proxy routeshigh

There is no rate limiting on /api/dealer/login, /forgot-password, the OTP endpoints, Places proxy or /img. Each login costs ≈ 0.6 CPU-seconds and one Resend email; a script can burn the email quota and the vCPU. Add per-IP limits (express-rate-limit or the DO WAF).

6
Cut the shop-status refreshhigh

Raise STATUS_CACHE_TTL_MS from 5 to 30–60 minutes and move Groq to the Developer tier; either license open-meteo commercially or swap in a free-for-commercial weather source. This also removes the “AI sentence” cost line from the model.

7
Confirm the database is a managed cluster and add a standbyhigh

Point-in-time recovery is 7 days on managed; a NZ$12 dev database has no backups. Keep the platform to one instance or add a pooler: managed 1 GB allows 22 connections and each platform instance opens up to 10.

8
Realtime is in-memorymedium

SSE clients live in a Map inside the process, so a second platform instance would split dealers and admins across two islands. Either keep the platform vertically scaled (recommended) or fan out through Postgres LISTEN/NOTIFY.

9
Per-route body limitsmedium

express.json({ limit: '100mb' }) applies before authentication on every route. Move the large limit to the two upload routes only.

10
Ephemeral filesmedium

Newsletter sign-ups append to subscribers.txt and the chatbot logs to disk inside the site container; both vanish on every deploy. Already tracked as the lead-capture gap; worth closing before the pilot because it is a visible data loss.

11
Housekeeping that grows with dealerslow

The boot-time profile rewrite (syncStoredDealerProfiles) took 4 s at 106 dealers and runs on every deploy; the image-cache warmer and the shop-status town table hardcode the three current dealers; the site’s host cache holds 200 entries. All fine at 100, all worth a note in the roadmap.

5

Onboarding-day drill (manual, staging)

Clone both DO apps to a staging app pair (doctl apps create --spec .do/app.yaml with new names and a copy of the database), then with three people: invite 20 dealers from the admin, activate 10 accounts within ten minutes, and have three of them upload 10 photos each at the same time while a fourth person reloads a dealer site every few seconds.

PASS every invite and OTP email arrives (watch the Resend dashboard for the daily cap), uploads complete, the platform does not restart (DO Insights), the dealer site keeps answering under 3 s, and the admin inbox shows all 20 onboarding requests.
6

What to check in the dashboards after any run

  • DigitalOcean → each app → Insights: CPU, memory, restarts, bandwidth used against the 150 GiB allowance.
  • DigitalOcean → Databases: confirm the cluster type, connections in use, backup schedule, PITR window.
  • Resend → Emails: delivered vs. bounced, daily count against the plan cap.
  • Groq console → Limits: requests per day used by the shop-status key.
  • Google Cloud → Maps Platform → Quotas: Places requests and spend.
