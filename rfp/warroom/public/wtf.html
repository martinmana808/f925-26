WTF?read before Thursday

STIHL answered our four questions in a day. The commercial answer is clear: optional, dealer pays, STIHL does nothing to drive it. But that answer does not fit the RFP they wrote two weeks ago, which has a contract, a pilot and a "network rollout". This page puts the two documents side by side, verbatim, and asks the question we still have to ask. Flag ⚑ or comment 💬 on any block.

1. Introduction (p.2)

"STIHL SHOP seeks proposals from experienced digital agencies and platform providers capable of delivering a managed dealer website framework aligned to the national STIHL SHOP digital strategy."
"Individual stores maintain their own websites to promote their local business […] These websites serve primarily as local marketing and information platforms […] They are not intended to function as e-commerce sites."

2. Project background (p.2–3)

"Dealer websites currently vary significantly across the network. Differences exist in: Website quality · User experience · Brand compliance · Mobile optimisation · Search engine performance · Content consistency · Promotion management · Technical support. This inconsistency creates customer confusion and reduces the effectiveness of the STIHL SHOP brand online."
"The purpose of this initiative is to establish a consistent national framework while allowing dealers some flexibility to personalise their local presence."

3. Project objectives (p.3)

"The successful vendor will assist STIHL SHOP to: Brand Consistency — Maintain a common STIHL SHOP look and feel across all dealer websites. Customer Experience — Deliver a premium digital experience consistent with stihlshop.co.nz. Dealer Flexibility — Allow dealers to personalise approved content areas. Governance — Provide central control of standards and brand compliance. Reduced Administration — Minimise website management requirements for dealers. Performance — Improve SEO, visibility, website speed and customer engagement."

4. Current digital environment — Dealer websites (p.4)

"For those that choose to have a website, dealers maintain local websites which: Promote their business · Provide workshop information · Promote local events · Display approved supplier products · Provide contact information · Promote local expertise."

Careful with this one: the section is titled Current Digital Environment. "Those that choose to have a website" describes who has one today — it is a description of the status quo, not a policy for the framework. The RFP's forward-looking sections (§3, §9, §19) all say "all dealer websites", "network-wide", "nationally consistent". Nothing in the RFP said the new framework would be optional.

5. Website governance model (p.4)

"National Ownership — STIHL SHOP will retain ownership of: Brand standards · Website structure · Templates · Navigation standards · STIHL product presentation · Digital compliance requirements · Approved supplier standards."
"Dealer Ownership — Dealers may maintain: Store information · Team profiles · Service capabilities · Community involvement · Local promotions · Approved supplier content. All local content must comply with STIHL SHOP standards."

9. Content management (p.6)

"The preferred vendor should provide a managed content service […] The supplier should be responsible for: Website content updates, including national promotions and campaigns · Seasonal campaign deployment across all dealer websites · Image and digital asset management · Promotional content updates · SEO optimisation and ongoing performance improvements · Brand compliance monitoring and reporting · Network-wide content updates and maintenance where changes apply across multiple or all dealer websites."

14. Implementation plan (p.8)

"Vendors should provide: Phase 1 – Discovery and platform design · Phase 2 – Pilot deployment · Phase 3 – Dealer onboarding · Phase 4 – Network rollout · Phase 5 – Ongoing optimisation and support."

16. Evaluation criteria · 18. Legal · 19. Desired outcome (p.8–9)

"Digital Strategy Alignment 20% · Design & Customer Experience 20% · Governance & Compliance 20% · Support Model 15% · Technical Capability 15% · Cost & Commercials 10%."
"STIHL SHOP will retain ownership of: Branding · Customer data · Dealer content · Digital assets."
"The preferred solution will provide a nationally consistent, professionally managed dealer website ecosystem that: Strengthens the STIHL SHOP brand · Complements stihlshop.co.nz · Provides dealers with local flexibility · Delivers a superior customer experience · Requires minimal dealer administration · Supports future growth of both STIHL and approved supplier brands."

2 · What Mark answeredVerbatim, Mark Ballantine, Retail Network Manager, 17 Sep 2026, replying to our letter of 15 Sep.

1. Brand standard or optional programme

"Dealer websites will be optional. Many STIHL SHOP dealers do not currently operate their own website and instead rely on the national website, stihlshop.co.nz, as their primary digital presence. Dealers who choose to have their own website may elect to use the approved website framework resulting from this RFP. Dealers will be responsible for all costs associated with the establishment, hosting, maintenance and ongoing management of their website through the selected vendor. STIHL New Zealand will not centrally contract or fund dealer websites. Vendors should therefore base their proposals on an opt-in model, with individual dealers engaging and paying for the service directly."

2. Existing sites

"Dealers who choose to adopt the approved website framework may elect to migrate content from their existing website. Vendors should outline their recommended migration approach, including any associated costs, responsibilities, and assumptions. Any migration services would be arranged and funded by the participating dealer."

3. Brand assets and STIHL sign-off

"STIHL New Zealand will provide access to approved STIHL SHOP logos and brand assets as required. Approved STIHL product imagery is already available to dealers through STIHL's internal dealer portal and may be used in accordance with existing guidelines. The proposed website framework should be developed in alignment with STIHL SHOP branding; however, STIHL New Zealand does not intend to undertake formal design certification or approval processes prior to pilot deployment. Vendors should demonstrate how their proposed solution aligns with STIHL SHOP brand standards as part of their submission. Any non-STIHL supplier imagery, content, or brand assets will need to be provided by the relevant dealer or supplier."

4. Cybersecurity standards

"STIHL New Zealand does not mandate a specific cybersecurity framework or certification. Vendors are expected to ensure their proposed solution complies with all applicable cybersecurity, privacy and data protection requirements and follows recognised industry best practices. Responsibility for maintaining appropriate security controls, monitoring, vulnerability management, backup and recovery processes, and ongoing compliance rests with the vendor. Vendors should outline any relevant certifications, standards or security frameworks they currently maintain as part of their proposal."

3 · The contradictionIt is not one we invented. It sits between their own RFP and their own answer.

What the RFP saysWhat Mark answered
Contract Finalisation — 13 NovemberSTIHL "will not centrally contract or fund dealer websites". So a contract between whom and whom? About what?
Pilot Store Deployment — DecemberA pilot with which dealers, paid by whom, evaluated by whom, against what success criteria? If the dealer pays and decides, that is not a pilot. That is a customer.
Network Rollout Commences — January 2027There is no network. There are individual dealers who might buy, whenever they like. A rollout is something somebody pushes.
"Across all dealer websites" · "central control" · "network-wide content updates" · "seasonal campaign deployment across all dealer websites"Only reachable if all dealers are on the framework. Under opt-in, "all" means "the ones who bought".
"A nationally consistent, professionally managed dealer website ecosystem" (§19 Desired Outcome) · "This inconsistency creates customer confusion" (§2)Consistency is the whole stated purpose of the RFP — and consistency needs many dealers on the same thing. If a handful opt in and the rest stay on whatever they have (or on nothing), the inconsistency the RFP opens with is untouched. So either STIHL intends to get most dealers onto it (which is a programme, whatever they call it) or the RFP cannot deliver its own desired outcome. Under the answer as given, we are being asked to invent a product for dealers, not to solve STIHL's stated problem.
Evaluation panel, weightings, shortlist, presentations 22–23 OctAll that apparatus to… recommend a supplier who then sells on their own.

An RFP with a panel, a contract, a pilot and a rollout is the process of a purchase. Yesterday's answer describes a recommendation. Both cannot be true at the same time.

4 · Three possible readingsWe cannot write the proposal without knowing which one it is.

Reading 1 — Mark answered the commercial model, not the programme

A framework agreement between vendor and STIHL may well exist (exclusivity, brand licence, term), a pilot STIHL nominates, and a rollout STIHL communicates — and "we don't fund" only means "the dealers pay the monthly fee". This is the optimistic reading, and it is plausible: he is the Retail Network Manager and answered from the dealer's angle.

Reading 2 — The RFP was written with an ambition that has since been walked back internally

Someone wrote "network rollout" thinking of a mandate; along the way they decided they cannot interfere with what each independent dealer does. The skeleton of the process is left without its content.

Reading 3 — It is a process to have an "approved supplier" on a list, and nothing more

Contract, pilot and rollout are words inherited from a template.

What it means either way

  • Guaranteed revenue from winning: $0. The addressable market is the dealers who already want a site — "many dealers do not currently operate their own website".
  • The network-programme pricing table is dead. Volume bands survive only as an incentive to dealers, not a lever for STIHL.
  • "F925 does not sell; adoption is STIHL's job" was right for a mandate and is wrong for this: under opt-in, if we don't create demand nobody does.
  • What the win is still worth: "approved supplier to STIHL SHOP dealers" on every pitch, a real pilot as a reference, and the badge for every other dealer network we go after next.
  • §6 has to stand alone (no standard to point at) — the I-group (monitoring, backups, restore drill) just became proposal-critical.

5 · The follow up we sendTheir own document, quoted back. Warm, obvious, no way out: either there is a programme (and they say which) or there is not (and they admit it in writing).

Follow up: the pilot, the contract and the rollout

Hi Mark,

Thank you for coming back so quickly. The commercial model is now clear: optional, dealer funded, dealer engaged.

Reading your answers against the RFP, we want to make sure we are responding to the right brief, because the two documents describe different things.

The RFP asks for a managed framework that maintains "a common STIHL SHOP look and feel across all dealer websites", with "central control of standards and brand compliance", "seasonal campaign deployment across all dealer websites" and "network wide content updates". Its desired outcome is "a nationally consistent, professionally managed dealer website ecosystem", and the problem it opens with is that today's inconsistency "creates customer confusion and reduces the effectiveness of the STIHL SHOP brand online". The timeline then has a contract in November, a pilot in December and a network rollout in January.

Your answer describes something else: individual dealers who may or may not choose a supplier, at their own cost, with no central involvement.

We can respond to either. But they are not the same proposal, and the second one cannot deliver the first one's outcome: consistency across the network only happens if most of the network is on the same framework. If a handful of dealers opt in and the rest stay where they are, the inconsistency the RFP sets out to fix is untouched.

So that we write the right proposal, could you help us with three things from the RFP timeline:

  • Contract finalisation, 13 November. If STIHL NZ does not contract for dealer websites, what is the agreement signed on that date between STIHL NZ and the vendor, and what does it cover: term, exclusivity, obligations on each side?
  • Pilot store deployment, December. Which dealers, nominated by whom, funded by whom, and what does STIHL NZ evaluate before the next phase?
  • Network rollout, January 2027. With adoption optional and dealer led, what does STIHL NZ mean by a network rollout, and what part does STIHL NZ play in it?

And, from the vendor's side, what the appointment actually consists of:

  • Exclusivity. Is the appointed vendor the sole approved website supplier for STIHL SHOP dealers, or one of several on a list?
  • Term. How long does the appointment last, and what happens at the end of it?
  • Introduction to dealers. Does STIHL NZ introduce the appointed vendor to the network in any way: a listing on stihlshop.co.nz or the dealer portal, a mention in dealer communications, a place in new dealer onboarding? Or is the vendor expected to find and approach each dealer on its own?
  • The pilot. Who pays for the December pilot, STIHL NZ or the participating dealers, and how many stores does STIHL NZ intend to put in it?

The proposal for an appointed, introduced supplier delivering a consistent network is a different document from the proposal for an option dealers might find on their own. We would rather write the one you actually want.
