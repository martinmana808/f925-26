Everything between those two sentences is achievable. Whether it actually happens is decided by one number: what it costs a dealer to say yes.

For the dealer

  • A STIHL SHOP website that looks like an extension of stihlshop.co.nz, live in five working days — and they will have seen it before they commit to anything.
  • Their STIHL BRANDKIT actually posted. The seasonal assets and monthly calendar STIHL already produces, published to their Facebook and Instagram, on brand and on schedule, without the dealer touching it (§5).
  • Local events and promotions they can put up themselves in about the time it takes to write a Facebook post — on the website and on their social at the same time.
  • A blog that works for search and for AI answers — written for them, about their town and their workshop, not generic filler.
  • Their in-store range on show — the approved-supplier products they actually stock, alongside STIHL categories that link through to stihlshop.co.nz.
  • An analytics dashboard in plain words — visitors, enquiries, where people came from — so they can see whether it is working.
  • They never touch code, hosting, design or compliance. We set up the domain and, if they need them, the Facebook and Instagram accounts — in their name, owned by them (§4.4).
  • Three tiers, one website. The site, the brand compliance and the support are identical at every price. What changes is how much we do each month.

For STIHL New Zealand

  • Zero cost and zero administration, at every stage including the pilot.
  • Brand governance enforced by the software on every site, at every tier — not reviewed by anyone at STIHL.
  • National campaigns pushed to every participating site in one action.
  • The BRANDKIT finally reaching customers. STIHL already pays to produce it every season. Its value is capped by how many dealers post it. We remove that cap (§5).
  • A dealer-ready introduction kit, so “promote the preferred partner” is an email STIHL forwards, not a project STIHL runs.
  • One page a quarter, so the two-year review answers itself.

01

What we understood from the brief

We have read the RFP of 4 September and your clarifications of 18 September as one brief. Here is what we heard, in STIHL SHOP’s own words, and what we think follows from it.

1.1 · The problem

Dealer websites “vary significantly across the network” in quality, user experience, brand compliance, mobile optimisation, search performance, content consistency, promotion management and technical support, and “this inconsistency creates customer confusion and reduces the effectiveness of the STIHL SHOP brand online”. The desired outcome is “a nationally consistent, professionally managed dealer website ecosystem” that strengthens the brand, complements stihlshop.co.nz, gives dealers local flexibility, delivers a superior customer experience and requires minimal dealer administration.

1.2 · The model, as confirmed

  • Dealer websites are optional. “Many STIHL SHOP dealers do not currently operate their own website and instead rely on the national website.”
  • Each participating dealer contracts with and pays the vendor directly. “STIHL New Zealand will not contract for, fund or manage dealer websites.” That includes the pilot.
  • STIHL New Zealand appoints one preferred website partner under a Memorandum of Understanding with an initial term of two years, setting out “the brand, compliance and governance requirements that apply to websites operating under the STIHL SHOP brand”.
  • The appointment is not exclusive — “STIHL New Zealand cannot prescribe which website provider a dealer must use” — but the appointed partner is “recognised and supported as STIHL New Zealand’s preferred website supplier”, and STIHL “will support and recommend the appointed preferred partner as the supplier best positioned to deliver a compliant STIHL SHOP website solution”.
  • STIHL New Zealand intends to actively introduce and promote the partner to the network, “through dealer communications and other appropriate engagements”.
  • Compliance applies regardless of provider. “Dealers who operate their own website must ensure their website complies with STIHL SHOP brand and compliance requirements, regardless of the provider they choose.”
  • A pilot with a small number of jointly selected dealers validates the framework before it is introduced more broadly, assessed on brand alignment, consistency, ease of implementation and management, vendor support and governance, and dealer feedback.
  • No mandated security framework and no formal design certification before the pilot. Responsibility for security controls, monitoring, vulnerability management, backup and recovery “rests with the vendor”.

1.3 · What follows from that

Consistency is an adoption problem before it is a design problem

A framework only makes a network consistent if most of the network is on it. Since nobody can be compelled and nobody will be funded, the preferred partner has exactly two jobs: make joining so easy and so cheap that a busy dealer says yes, and make sure that once they have joined the brand is protected without anyone at STIHL having to police it. Section 2 is why we changed the price. Section 3 is how dealers say yes. Sections 4 to 9 are how we keep them, and the brand, safe once they have.

1.4 · The boundaries we respect

  • Dealer sites are local marketing and information sites. No e-commerce. Any STIHL product journey redirects to the relevant page on stihlshop.co.nz (RFP §6). Tauranga already works this way.
  • STIHL SHOP owns brand standards, website structure, templates, navigation standards, STIHL product presentation, digital compliance requirements and approved-supplier standards (§5 of the RFP). The template is built so a dealer cannot change any of them.
  • Dealers own store information, team profiles, service capabilities, community involvement, local promotions and approved-supplier content. Those are exactly the fields the dealer portal exposes, and nothing else.
  • STIHL SHOP owns branding, customer data, dealer content and digital assets (RFP §18). Our agreement with each dealer says so, and every dealer can export everything at any time.
02

The only lever we have

This section is the strategy, and it is the one place where we say something that was not in the RFP. It is short, and everything else in this document depends on it.

2.1 · A dealer website is worth different amounts to different people

To an individual dealer, a website is worth a modest and local amount: the phone rings a little more often, the workshop turns up on Google Maps, the opening hours are right at Labour Weekend, somebody else deals with it. Real, but small. Most STIHL SHOP owners are running a shop and a workshop, not a marketing department, which is exactly why — as your answer says — many of them have never bothered to get one.

To STIHL SHOP as a brand, ninety consistent dealer websites are worth something else entirely, and the RFP lists it: brand consistency, a premium customer experience matching stihlshop.co.nz, national campaigns deployed everywhere in one action, unified messaging, search presence in every town in New Zealand, and the end of the customer confusion §2 of the RFP opens with.

The value sits mostly with the brand. The invoice sits entirely with the dealer. That is not a criticism of the model — it is a fact about the model, and it is the single best predictor of how much of the network ends up on the framework. Priced like a normal website build and retainer, adoption stays in single digits and the RFP cannot reach its own stated outcome.

That is the argument. The next section is the evidence for it, on both sides: what a New Zealand customer now does before they visit a shop, and what is actually sitting on the STIHL SHOP network today.

2.2 · What a website is worth to a dealer, and what a bad one costs

“A modest amount” is not good enough if we are asking a dealer to sign something, so here are the numbers we would put in front of one. They come in three parts: what the customer now does, what is actually on the network today, and what we will and will not promise.

2.3 · We are not asking STIHL to fix that with money

We accept the commercial model exactly as you have set it out. We are not asking STIHL New Zealand to fund dealer websites, to subsidise the pilot, or to carry any cost or administration. We have done two things instead: we have taken the mismatch out of the price, and we have made the dealer’s monthly fee buy something they can see working without doing anything — their STIHL campaigns, live on their own Facebook and Instagram, every month (§5).

2.4 · Why we can do that — the cost of the fortieth dealer

The reason most website agencies cannot offer a compliant, maintained, monitored website for a hundred dollars a month is that every site they run is a separate project: separate code, separate hosting, separate updates, separate person. Ours is not.

  • One application serves every dealer. Every dealer domain points at the same site application, which loads that dealer’s content by hostname. Adding a dealer costs a DNS record and a database row — not a build.
  • One deploy updates everybody. A template improvement, a security patch, a brand change or a national campaign reaches every participating site at once, so the work does not multiply with the network.
  • Onboarding is a wizard, not a design project. The dealer answers a structured set of questions once; the template does the rest. That is what makes five working days a normal timeframe rather than a rush.
  • We can build a dealer’s site before they ask. We assemble a working preview from public information — the STIHL SHOP store locator, their Google Business listing, their existing site if they have one. We have already done this across the STIHL SHOP network, so a first draft costs us very little.
  • Compliance is checked by software. Template version, legal pages, product links resolving to stihlshop.co.nz, image alt text, broken links and performance budget are checked automatically on every site, so governance does not consume a person per dealer either.
  • The infrastructure cost per dealer at network scale is a few dollars a month. Load-tested in September 2026 against a copy of the platform seeded with 106 dealers: the public API sustains roughly 120 requests a second where a hundred dealers need about three, and the dealer-site tier sustains roughly 35 pages a second against an average load near 0.4. The headroom is already paid for.

So the marginal cost of the fortieth dealer is close to the marginal cost of the fourth. What genuinely costs money in this business is human attention: writing content, running promotions, choosing and scheduling the right posts, answering the phone. That is the honest shape of our cost base, and it is why the tiers in §13 are built around how much of that attention a dealer gets each month — not around holding features back.

Put plainly: the entry tier is not a discount and it is not an introductory offer. It is what the software actually costs to run once it runs for a network instead of for one customer. We would rather have sixty dealers on brand at a hundred dollars than six at six hundred, and so, we think, would STIHL SHOP.

03

How dealers say yes

A dealer is a shop owner with a workshop to run. They adopt the framework if three things are true: it is obviously better than what they have, it costs them almost no time, and STIHL has told them it is the right one. We take care of the first two and make the third as easy as forwarding an email.

3.1 · The offer a dealer hears

“This is your STIHL SHOP website. It is on brand, it is already built, and it goes live five working days after you say yes. Every month we put STIHL’s seasonal campaigns on your Facebook and Instagram for you, keep your hours and promotions right, and write about your workshop so people in your town find you. From a hundred dollars a month, everything included. Recommended by STIHL New Zealand.”

Every part of that is load-bearing. Already built, because of §3.2 — the dealer is not being asked to imagine anything. Five working days, because the wizard collects the rest and the template does the work. Every month, because the thing a dealer can actually feel is their name appearing where their customers already are, and that starts in the first month at every tier. Recommended by STIHL New Zealand, because that is what the appointment gives us the right to say, and it is candidly the part of this we cannot manufacture ourselves.

3.2 · See it before you buy it

The biggest reason a dealer says no to a website is not price. It is that they cannot picture it, and every quote they have ever had asked them to. So we remove the imagination step entirely.

When STIHL introduces a dealer to us, or a dealer gets in touch, the first thing we send back is not a quote or a questionnaire. It is their site: a working preview built from public information — their STIHL SHOP store locator entry, their Google Business listing, their existing website if they have one. Their shop, their hours, their town, their photographs, on the STIHL SHOP template. No form, no commitment, nothing to install, and no obligation to reply. If they like it, the onboarding wizard fills in what public information cannot know and the preview becomes the live site. If they do not, they have lost nothing and neither have we.

This is why five working days is a normal timeframe and not a promise we have to sprint to keep: by the time a dealer says yes, most of the work is already done and they have already seen it. build: preview-to-live flow · X-06

3.3 · Five working days, with nothing needed from the dealer

A five-day promise is worth nothing if it quietly depends on the dealer finding something. In practice that is exactly where these projects die: nobody knows who the registrar is, the login belongs to a web developer who stopped answering in 2019, the DNS sits in an account set up by a staff member who has left, and a job that was going to take a week takes four months and three apologetic emails. The dealers most likely to fall into that hole are precisely the ones this framework needs most.

So we have designed the dealer out of the critical path. We register the domain, we run the DNS, we build the site, and it goes live. Nothing is waiting on the dealer finding a password. Then we hand the ownership to them.

The naming convention already exists — it is simply not complete

We are not proposing to invent an address scheme. One is already in use across most of the network; nobody has ever owned it or finished it. Of the 57 dealer websites we found, 41 already use stihlshop<town>.co.nz. Another six are near-misses on the same idea — .com instead of .co.nz, .nz instead of .co.nz, or the words reversed. And 83 of the 93 outlets already use a @stihlshop.co.nz email address, so STIHL already operates exactly this kind of shared identity namespace for the network. Websites are the one place it was never applied.

On 23 September 2026 we ran the convention name for all 93 outlets against the official .nz register. The namespace is in better shape than we expected, and it will not stay that way:

The other 14 are the warning. Some are a dealer group sensibly securing names for its own branches. Some are the dealer’s own name sitting on the wrong extension while a stranger holds the right one. And several are plainly parked — one created in June 2024 and pointed at a domain-parking service, others dormant on parking hosts since 2012 and 2014. Two more were registered as recently as May and August 2026. The namespace is being picked off slowly, by nobody in particular, and every name that goes is one the network does not get back cheaply. We would rather hand STIHL a list of what is still available than explain in two years why it is not.

Completing that pattern is worth something on its own, separately from anything else in this proposal. A customer who learns that a STIHL SHOP lives at stihlshop<their town>.co.nz can find any of the other ninety-two without a search engine. That is a small piece of national brand infrastructure that STIHL does not have today and cannot easily assemble later.

What we would do

  • Dealers with no website (35 of 93). We register the domain to the convention, host it, build the site and go live. The dealer supplies content through the wizard and nothing else. Five working days is unconditional here, because there is no legacy to negotiate with.
  • Dealers with an existing site. Their new site goes live on the convention domain on the same five-day timetable, so they are never blocked. Where their current domain has real history behind it — and for a ten-year-old local domain it usually does — their domain then becomes the primary address as soon as access is available, with the convention domain redirecting to it.
