What are we bidding for?for discussion · nothing decided

The RFP does not say whether the winner gets the network or a recommendation. That single unknown changes the business more than anything else in the bid — so this page lays out the possible answers as a tree, and then one idea for how pricing could be shaped so that we are ready for whichever branch STIHL's answer lands on. Flag ⚑ or comment 💬 on any node; nothing here goes into the proposal until the three of us agree, and the final shape of §9 depends on what STIHL answers to CQ-01 (asked 18 Sep, answers expected ~25 Sep).

How to read it: the first question has two answers; each of those has two ways of paying for it. Four end-states. What we would earn, what we would have to do, and what we would write in the proposal is different in each — and the pricing idea below is an attempt to have one answer that works in all four.

What the RFP itself suggests

Nobody runs a formal RFP with a weighted evaluation panel, a pilot in December and a rollout in January to write a recommendation letter — that is a procurement instrument for a programme. And the problem STIHL says it is solving is brand inconsistency across ~90 dealer sites; a recommendation does not fix inconsistency, only a standard does. So the intent is probably the right-hand side of the tree. What is likely still undecided on their side is level 2 — who pays — and that is the part we can influence.

Cheapest evidence available: the STIHL SHOP dealer agreement already says what head office can require (fit-out, signage, brand standards). If digital presence is in there, or could be, a website standard is contractually available to them and the question becomes "when", not "whether". Worth checking from the dealer side before Thursday.

The pricing ideaAn idea, not a decision: one product, two ways to buy it — so the same proposal works on every branch of the tree.

One product, two ways to buy it

Every dealer site is the same all-inclusive managed service; that does not change. What changes the price is not what the dealer gets but the commitment behind it: dealers engaging one at a time, or STIHL adopting the framework as a network standard.

Why the programme rate follows the commitment, not the payerThe one design choice that makes the idea work on the "dealer pays" branches.

1 · Don't tie the discount to who pays

The tempting version is "if head office pays, $350; if the dealer pays, $600." That version breaks on Scenario B: a mandated cost that the dealer did not choose, at full price, is the outcome that dealers will resent — and it puts STIHL in the position of forcing a cost on its own network.

The alternative: the programme rate applies whenever STIHL adopts the framework as a standard, regardless of whose name is on the invoice. A mandate then makes every dealer cheaper, not just head office. That gives STIHL something to say to dealers — "we made the website a brand standard and negotiated you 40% off" — instead of "we are forcing a cost on you". A standard that makes dealers cheaper is easy politics; a standard that only saves head office money is hard politics. It also keeps "who pays" as a second-level question, which is what it is.

2 · Don't say 50% — say why the price drops

An evaluation panel with Cost at 10% and Strategy at 20% will read "$600 or $300" as either a penalty on dealers or padding on the top price. The gap has to be explained by cost that genuinely disappears when the network moves together:

  • no per-dealer selling or negotiation — STIHL hands us dealers ready to onboard
  • the template certified once with STIHL marketing, instead of re-explained ninety times
  • one contract and one relationship instead of ninety
  • no churn, no collections work
  • a rollout we can schedule in waves instead of react to

Those are worth 35–45%. 50% would look like a bluff, and would invite the question of why the dealer-direct price is so high.

3 · Volume bands are the same lever for the optional case

If STIHL will not mandate, the dealer-direct table steps down for everyone as the network grows: $600 up to 20 dealers live, $500 at 21–50, $400 at 51+. That gives STIHL a reason to push adoption even without a mandate, gives early dealers a reason to recruit the others, and quietly says: a mandate is just everyone at the best rate on day one.

So a 40% lower rate is not a discount; it is the better business — for us (guaranteed revenue, one relationship, schedulable work) and for STIHL (control, consistency, cheaper dealers). The trade STIHL will ask for in return is on ownership and exit (CQ-04): data export, a defined handover, possibly escrow. Ninety-two guaranteed dealers in exchange for exit guarantees is a good trade; losing the mandate to keep every scrap of IP is a bad one.

What is not decidedEverything on this page is for discussion. These are the open points.

  • Whether to offer two tables at all. The alternative is one price and a note that "a network-level structure is available on request" — safer, less persuasive.
  • The programme figures. $900 / $350 are a shape, not a number. From the dealer side: what would a dealer accept as "a good deal my head office got me"? From the cost side: does the 100-dealer cost model still clear at $350? (capacity report)
  • Whether bands belong in the dealer-direct table or only in the internal fallback.
  • What we are willing to give for the mandate — the CQ-04 trade (exit, data, escrow). This should be decided before STIHL answers, not after.
  • Timing. Questions go to STIHL 18 Sep; answers ~25 Sep; proposal due 9 Oct. §9 can be finalised in the week of 28 Sep with the answer in hand. Until then §9 in the draft carries both tables marked "proposed".
