The short version

STIHL SHOP’s problem is inconsistency. It can only be fixed by adoption. No dealer can be made to move, so the preferred partner has to be the one dealers want to move to — and the one STIHL can trust with the brand once they have. This proposal is built around those two jobs.

For the dealer

  • A STIHL SHOP website that looks like an extension of stihlshop.co.nz, live in five working days from the day they say yes.
  • They never touch code, hosting or design. They answer a guided wizard once; after that they ask for changes in plain English and we make them.
  • $2,500 setup, $600 a month, everything included. Cheaper for everyone as more dealers join.
  • Their own traffic numbers, in plain words, inside the portal they already use.

For STIHL New Zealand

  • Zero cost, zero administration. STIHL sets the standards; the platform enforces them.
  • National campaigns pushed to every participating site in one action. Brand compliance monitored and reported, not hoped for.
  • A dealer-ready introduction kit, so “promoting the preferred partner” is one email STIHL forwards, not a project STIHL runs.
  • A quarterly network report — adoption, compliance, uptime, support — so the two-year review answers itself.

What is already real

This is not a proposal to build a platform. UNIFY already runs STIHL SHOP Tauranga in production: the dealer portal, the onboarding wizard, the change-request workflow, the helpdesk with dealer approval, error tracking, per-dealer analytics, and the social-posting tool that published its first post to a STIHL dealer’s Facebook and Instagram on 16 September. The pilot in December runs on software that exists today.

01

What we understood from the brief

We read the RFP and the clarifications of 17 September as one brief. Here is what we heard, in STIHL’s own terms, and what it means for how a partner should behave.

1.1 · The problem

Dealer websites “vary significantly across the network” in quality, experience, brand compliance, mobile, SEO, content, promotions and support, and “this inconsistency creates customer confusion and reduces the effectiveness of the STIHL SHOP brand online”. The desired outcome is “a nationally consistent, professionally managed dealer website ecosystem” that complements stihlshop.co.nz, gives dealers local flexibility and needs minimal dealer administration.

1.2 · The model

  • Dealer websites are optional. Many dealers rely on stihlshop.co.nz alone today.
  • Each participating dealer contracts with and pays the vendor directly. STIHL New Zealand does not contract for, fund or manage dealer websites.
  • STIHL New Zealand appoints one preferred website partner under a two-year MOU, sets brand, compliance and governance requirements, provides brand assets, and actively introduces and promotes the partner to the network.
  • Any dealer with a website must comply with STIHL SHOP brand and compliance requirements, whichever provider they use.
  • A pilot with a small number of jointly selected dealers validates the framework before it is introduced to the wider network.

1.3 · What follows from that

Consistency is an adoption problem before it is a design problem

A framework only makes the network consistent if most of the network is on it. Since nobody can be compelled, the preferred partner’s first job is to make joining easy, fast and obviously worthwhile for a busy dealer — and the second is to make sure that once a dealer has joined, the brand is protected without STIHL having to police it. Everything in this proposal is organised around those two jobs: Section 2 is how dealers say yes; Sections 3 to 7 are how we keep them, and the brand, safe once they have.

1.4 · The boundaries we will respect

  • Dealer sites are local marketing and information sites. No e-commerce. Any STIHL product journey redirects to the relevant page on stihlshop.co.nz (RFP §6). We already do this on Tauranga.
  • STIHL SHOP owns brand standards, structure, templates, navigation, STIHL product presentation, compliance requirements and approved-supplier standards (§5). The template is built so a dealer cannot change those areas.
  • Dealers own store information, team, services, community, local promotions and approved-supplier content (§5). Those are exactly the fields the dealer portal exposes.
  • STIHL owns branding, customer data, dealer content and digital assets (§18). Our contract with each dealer says so, and every dealer can export everything at any time.
02

How dealers say yes — the adoption plan

This section is the strategy. A dealer is a shop owner with a workshop to run. They will adopt the framework if three things are true: it is clearly better than what they have, it costs them almost no time, and STIHL has told them it is the right one. We take care of the first two and make the third as easy as forwarding an email.

2.1 · The offer a dealer hears

“Your STIHL SHOP website, on brand, live in five working days. You answer a few questions once; we do everything else, forever. $2,500 to set up, $600 a month, nothing extra. Recommended by STIHL New Zealand.”

Every word of that is load-bearing. Five working days because the wizard collects everything and the template does the rest. Once because after onboarding the dealer only ever asks for changes. Nothing extra because hosting, SSL, backups, content changes, SEO and support are inside the monthly fee (§11). Recommended by STIHL New Zealand because that is what the MOU gives us the right to say.

2.2 · See it before you buy it

The single biggest reason a dealer says no to a website is not price; it is not being able to picture it. So we remove the imagination step. For any dealer STIHL introduces, we build a preview of their site from public information (their Google Business listing, their current site if they have one, their stihlshop.co.nz store page) and send them the link. No commitment, no form. If they like it, the wizard fills in the rest and the preview becomes the site. build: preview-from-public-data flow · runbook X-06

2.3 · The introduction kit we hand STIHL

Mark said STIHL “intends to actively introduce and promote the appointed preferred website partner”. We will make that a one-hour job for STIHL, not a campaign. On appointment we deliver:

  • The dealer one-pager (PDF and web): what they get, what it costs, three screenshots, how to start. STIHL SHOP co-branded, STIHL-approved before it goes anywhere.
  • Three ready-to-send dealer emails for STIHL’s dealer communications: the announcement, the pilot results, the “your site must comply — here is the assessed option” reminder.
  • A page on our side (e.g. stihlshop-websites.co.nz) that STIHL can link from the dealer portal or any communication: the offer, the preview request, the pilot dealers’ sites as live examples. domain + page — decide name
  • A slot in new-dealer onboarding: one paragraph and a link for STIHL’s new-dealer pack, so every dealer who joins the network from 2027 hears about it on day one.
  • A 15-minute walkthrough we run for STIHL’s retail team so they can answer the first dealer questions themselves.

We will never use that message aggressively — the tone is here is the easy way, not you are non-compliant — but it belongs in the kit, in STIHL’s voice.

04

How it stays on brand — governance

Governance is 20% of the evaluation and, under an opt-in model, the thing STIHL is actually relying on the partner for. Ours is enforced by the software, evidenced by an audit trail, and reported quarterly.

4.1 · National ownership, enforced

  • Templates, navigation, STIHL product presentation and compliance elements live in code we control and deploy to every site at once. A dealer cannot alter them.
  • National promotions and seasonal campaigns are deployed by us across all participating sites in one action, on STIHL’s calendar, with STIHL’s assets. Regional or tiered campaigns are supported. campaigns module: national push exists; scheduling UI — task B-05
  • Brand assets are served from one library; when STIHL updates a logo, every site updates.

4.2 · Dealer content, approved before it is live

Every dealer change goes through the portal as a change request. Deterministic changes (hours, phone, a price on a supplier product) are applied by us within the content SLA. Anything with judgement in it — copy, imagery, a new page section — is reviewed by us against STIHL SHOP standards before it is published; if we are unsure, we ask STIHL. Nothing a dealer types appears on a live site unreviewed.

4.3 · The audit trail

Every change request, approval, publish and support ticket is time-stamped with who asked, who reviewed and what changed. For any site, at any time, STIHL can be shown exactly how it came to look the way it does. This is the record that makes “brand compliance monitoring and reporting” (§9) a report we run, not a manual check.

4.4 · Compliance monitoring and reporting

  • Automated checks on every site: template version, legal pages present, STIHL product links resolving to stihlshop.co.nz, no local STIHL pricing, image alt text, broken links, performance budget.
  • A quarterly network compliance report to STIHL (§14): sites live, checks passed, exceptions and how they were resolved.
  • Disputes over a dealer’s local content: we hold the change, refer it to STIHL’s nominated contact, and apply STIHL’s ruling. We never publish over STIHL’s objection.

4.5 · Approved-supplier content

Dealers may promote non-STIHL products and services relevant to their business (§7). The portal has a dedicated supplier-products area, kept visually separate from STIHL product presentation, with the same review step. Supplier imagery and brand assets are the dealer’s or supplier’s to provide, as Mark confirmed.

Support model

One place for everything. A dealer never emails a person and hopes; they open the portal, say what they need, and can see where it is.

5.1 · How a dealer gets help

Request a change

For content. Field-by-field, mapped to the website, with a visible status from submitted to live. Deterministic changes applied within the content SLA; creative changes come back as a preview link to approve first.

For anything else. A ticket with a number, a thread, attachments, and a status the dealer can read: New → In progress → Waiting on you / Please test → Resolved → Closed. 06

Hosting, security and continuity

Mark confirmed that STIHL mandates no specific security framework and that responsibility rests with the vendor. We hold no ISO certification and will not pretend otherwise. What we offer instead is a specific list of controls, the evidence for each, and a standing invitation to audit them.

6.1 · Architecture

  • One platform, many sites: every dealer site is served by the same application, keyed by domain. One fix fixes everyone; one campaign reaches everyone.
  • Hosted on DigitalOcean (Sydney region) behind Cloudflare’s edge for TLS, caching and DDoS protection. Data stays in Australia/NZ.
  • Media on object storage with CDN delivery; images resized at upload to every size a device needs.
  • Target topology for the programme: two production instances per tier plus a staging environment, so a failed instance is invisible to visitors and every change is rehearsed before it reaches a dealer. staging + second instance: planned for pilot, not yet built — task I-xmu0hw5a2

6.3 · Access and data protection

  • Dealer and admin logins require a password and a one-time code by email. No shared accounts.
  • Every write to a dealer’s data is scoped to that dealer at the database query; there is no path from one dealer’s login to another dealer’s content.
  • Secrets live in the hosting platform’s encrypted configuration, never in code. Third-party keys are rotated on staff change.
  • Contact-form submissions are stored first-party in New Zealand-adjacent infrastructure, delivered to the dealer, and never shared with a third-party form service.
  • Privacy Act 2020 compliance: per-dealer privacy policy, cookie consent that actually gates trackers, data export and deletion on request.

6.4 · How we build

Every change to the platform is a pull request: automated tests, a production build and an automated code review must pass, then a second engineer reviews before merge. Nothing reaches a dealer on one person’s say-so. We are happy to show STIHL the actual review history.

Analytics — dealer level and network level

The RFP asks for both. Dealer-level is live; network-level is the report that makes STIHL’s two-year review easy.

7.1 · For the dealer: “Your website”

Inside the portal, in plain words: people who visited, first-time visitors, pages looked at, how many got in touch, visitors per day, the pages people looked at most, how they found you (Google, typed the address, Facebook and Instagram), phone or computer. Seven, thirty or ninety days, with change against the previous period. No dashboards to learn. Google Analytics 4 underneath, set up by us, one property per site.

7.2 · For STIHL: the network view

A quarterly report (and, on request, a live view) across all participating sites: dealers live, visits, contacts generated, top local pages, search visibility trend, compliance checks passed, uptime, support volumes and response times. Aggregated by default; per-dealer on request with the dealer’s consent. network report: data exists per dealer; the aggregate report is to build before the pilot review — task R-05

7.3 · SEO

Local SEO is built into the template: structured data for the store (LocalBusiness, opening hours, reviews), per-dealer sitemaps, canonical handling, fast pages, and a monthly local article per dealer written by us and approved by the dealer. Technical SEO is a platform concern, fixed once for everyone. Keyword and traffic reporting sit in the dealer’s analytics; conversion means contact-form submissions and click-throughs to stihlshop.co.nz, both counted.

08

Migration approach

Mark asked vendors to outline the recommended approach, costs, responsibilities and assumptions. Migration is arranged and funded by the participating dealer.

8.1 · Approach

  1. Audit (included, before the dealer commits): we look at the existing site and tell the dealer which tier applies and what will carry over.
  2. Content carry-over: text and images the dealer wants to keep are brought into the wizard for them; anything STIHL-product-related is replaced by links to stihlshop.co.nz.
  3. Redirect map: every old URL that had search traffic redirects to its new equivalent, so local search rankings survive the move.
  4. Domain: the site launches on the dealer’s existing domain. We either receive delegated DNS access or send the dealer a two-line change to make; a domain the dealer does not own yet we register in the dealer’s name at cost.
  5. Cutover: new site live on a preview address first, dealer approves, DNS switched, old site kept read-only for 30 days.

8.2 · Tiers and costs
