01

Executive verdict

This RFP describes the platform we already built. Managed framework, locked brand, dealer flexibility inside approved areas, no dealer e-commerce, purchase journeys redirected to stihlshop.co.nz — section by section, the RFP reads like a spec of UNIFY's architecture. Of 84 assessed requirement points, 43 are already met or exceeded. That is a winning starting position nobody bidding a fresh WordPress build can match.

But we are not the favourite — we are the challenger

Little Rocket is not just "a competitor studio." Their own case study says they built a custom WordPress theme rolled out across 10 STIHL SHOP franchise websites, with a monthly retail campaign pipeline run with The Ideas Company. They are the incumbent. This RFP was very likely written with them in the room. We do not win by matching them — we win by making the evaluation about the things a 10-site WordPress operation cannot do: one codebase, measured performance, governed self-service, network-wide campaign deployment in one deploy, and a pilot that is already live.

The three battles that decide this bid

1 · Governance & Compliance (20%) — our approval workflow is genuinely superior, but nothing is documented, and the legal surface (privacy policy, data ownership, the chatbot's "I'm not an AI" instruction) currently works against us. Highest-leverage fix.

2 · Support Model (15%) — the RFP wants a 2-hour critical response matrix, escalation, and an emergency process. We publicly offer one number: 48-hour content changes. This is our weakest scored category and it is pure documentation + ops commitment to fix.

3 · Claims vs reality — the /unify page promises daily backups, 24/7 monitoring, and 99.9% uptime; none of that is implemented today. An evaluator who checks — and this one will, they wrote a 9-page RFP — must find every claim true. We make the claims true before we submit, not after.

Ammunition — the corporate site, measured

  • Mobile Lighthouse 19/100; CLS 0.442 — 4× over Google's 0.1 "good" threshold, on both mobile and desktop.
  • Its own server-timing header self-reports a 3.0 s origin first byte; Magento marks the HTML UNCACHEABLE.
  • 241 images and 80 scripts on the homepage; one merged CSS file of 683 KB raw.
  • The LCP hero image is itself loading="lazy" — a textbook anti-pattern.
  • og:site_name is "Main Website Store" — the unedited Magento default, visible in every social share.
  • CSP exists but is report-only with bare * wildcards — decorative, not protective.

Use with care: we complement this site, we don't trash it. Frame as "the dealer layer should protect the brand where the national platform can't move fast."

Ammunition — Little Rocket's own site, measured

  • Mobile Lighthouse 38/100; FCP 8.6 s; LCP 15.6 s; TTFB 3.3 s.
  • WordPress with generator meta exposed, x-powered-by: PHP/8.2.33 leaking, zero security headers, no CDN evidence, jQuery served with no cache-control at all.
  • 35 stylesheets + 46 scripts on the homepage; zero structured data.
  • Their WP user sitemap allows author enumeration.

This is the stack and practice pattern their 10 dealer sites inherit. We never name it in the proposal — we let a "minimum technical standards" appendix (security headers, CWV thresholds, structured data) do the scoring for us.

Claim integrity — fix before anyone else measures us

The pilot's homepage ships broken structured data: the live HTML contains the literal text {localBusinessSchema} — an un-interpolated template variable where the LocalBusiness JSON-LD should be. The schema exists in the code (+page.svelte) but never renders in production. Trivial fix, terrible look in a technical evaluation of a vendor selling SEO.

The "96/100 mobile" claim did not reproduce in these runs (66 from this vantage; the LCP animation bug alone explains most of it). After fixing P0-08, re-measure from NZ and only then put the number in the proposal — with methodology stated.

Also measured on the pilot: zero security headers (no HSTS/CSP/X-Frame-Options — the corporate site has HSTS; we look worse); homepage lab transfer 9.7 MiB, driven by five hero MP4s (26 MiB on disk, empty poster, no preload strategy) and dealer-uploaded 450 KB raw JPEGs that bypass the AVIF/WebP pipeline; cache-control: private / cf-cache-status: BYPASS on HTML while the marketing copy says "edge-cached worldwide."

What measured genuinely well: CLS 0.000–0.02 (the feared 0.13 carousel shift did not reproduce — the CSS work held); TTFB 5–6× faster than corporate and Little Rocket; immutable 1-year caching on built assets with Cloudflare HITs; correct 301 HTTP→HTTPS; valid certs; brotli everywhere; 24 responsive <picture> elements with AVIF+WebP.

Hosting truth, settled by measurement: production is SvelteKit (adapter-node) on DigitalOcean App Platform behind Cloudflare — not Netlify. The repo's netlify.toml and its cache-header rules are dead config. The proposal's hosting model section should say Cloudflare edge + DigitalOcean origin, and the repo should be cleaned to match (P1-18).

The war room · Little Rocket & the network

Assume Little Rocket writes a good bid. They are a real studio (New Plymouth, founder-led, 20+ years) with the two things we lack: incumbency and relationships. Their case study claims a custom WordPress theme across ten STIHL SHOP franchise sites, "each uniquely styled to reflect the store's local identity while maintaining brand consistency," plus monthly retail campaign production with The Ideas Company. Their bid, predicted

  • Incumbency: "we already run 10 of your dealer sites — lowest-risk path is to extend what works."
  • Relationships: dealers who know them; an existing campaign pipeline with The Ideas Company.
  • Familiarity: WordPress — a platform head office staff may feel they understand.
  • Local identity: "each site uniquely styled" — they'll pitch flexibility as a feature.

Their structural weaknesses

  • Per-site economics: 88 WordPress installs = 88 update queues, 88 plugin stacks, 88 attack surfaces. Seasonal campaign = 88 manual deployments (C3/C8 are our proof of the opposite).
  • "Uniquely styled per store" is the disease this RFP exists to cure — §2 lists brand inconsistency as the core problem. Quote their own case study language against the RFP's objectives, without naming them.
  • No visible platform, portal, analytics, SLA, or performance story. Their own site measures mobile 38/100, no security headers, no CDN, exposed PHP version, zero structured data.
  • Small like us — incumbency doesn't answer the continuity question any better than we do.

Our kill points (never name them; let standards do it)

  • Propose a minimum technical standards appendix: Core Web Vitals thresholds, security headers, structured-data validation, uptime SLA, WCAG target. We pass; a WordPress fleet mostly can't.
  • Demand-side demo: national promo published once → live on N demo sites instantly → auto-expires. The 88-deployment alternative refutes itself.
  • Governance mechanics: per-field approval + audit trail vs "please don't edit the header, we wrote it down."
  • Measured performance vs their measurable performance — invite the evaluators to run Lighthouse on anything, ours or anyone's.
  • The economics: one codebase amortised across the network vs 88 × maintenance-hours; our flat fee replaces the per-site retainer model entirely.

Where to respect them

  • Campaign production capability is real — our answer is the promotions module (gap #4) plus a content partnership stance, not dismissal.
